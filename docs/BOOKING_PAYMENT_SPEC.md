# Milestone 2 — Booking and payment domain specification

**Status:** Implementation contract for the fictional, Stripe test-mode portfolio. Commercial terms remain subject to Lithuanian legal review. This document defines behavior; it creates no schema or live-booking authority. See [state machines](BOOKING_STATE_MACHINES.md), [acceptance scenarios](BOOKING_ACCEPTANCE_SCENARIOS.md), and [decision register](DECISIONS.md).

## 1. Authority and terminology

The approved terms in `PROJECT_BRIEF.md` and `DECISIONS.md` govern. A **unit** is one of the three individually bookable cottages. A **booking** is the guest's contractual reservation record; a **hold** is a temporary claim on dates; a **payment attempt** is one provider checkout for an exact amount; an **assessment** is the documented calculation of a cancellation charge. Payment, booking, hold, assessment, refund, and notification states are independent.

| Approved business policy | 30% advance if booked more than 14 days before arrival; otherwise 100%; balance due at T–14; T–21 warning; T–14 notice; 24-hour grace; free guest cancellation strictly before T–14; at/after T–14 supportable charge capped at 30%; provider-attributable cancellation refunds all guest payments. |

No legal conclusion beyond the brief is asserted here. Stripe-specific design below follows [Checkout Session creation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create), [expiration](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/expire), and [webhook delivery guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks).

## 2. Inventory, dates, eligibility, and availability

Each unit has capacity and configurable booking rules. One unit-date interval is indivisible inventory; guest count must be positive and within its configured maximum. Store arrival and departure as local calendar **dates**, require `arrival < departure`, and count nights as calendar dates in `[arrival, departure)`. Departure day can be the next guest's arrival day. Intervals overlap exactly when `a.arrival < b.departure AND b.arrival < a.departure`; equality at either end does not overlap. Operator blocks use the same interval rule. Validate dates, capacity, minimum/maximum stay, enabled unit, approved price configuration and terms version server-side; no client calendar is authoritative. Same-day arrivals or lead-time limits need a later explicit rule rather than an invented prohibition.

Search availability against confirmed/active bookings, active checkout holds including session creation and reconciliation, and operator blocks. Availability reads are advisory. Acquisition or confirmation must run inside a PostgreSQL transaction with a database-enforced no-overlap invariant per unit (for example, a GiST exclusion constraint over a `daterange` and the set of inventory-blocking records, or an equivalent serialized inventory-claim table). Milestone 3 chooses exact SQL. The invariant must cover *both* holds and bookings in one conflict domain, not separate constraints that permit hold versus booking overlap. Include operator blocks. State changes that add, remove, or transfer a claim are atomic; a losing concurrent request receives an unavailable result. Never expose an expired claim as free until its payment state has been reconciled or it is placed in a safe blocking reconciliation state.

## 3. Time and money conventions

All instants are stored as UTC instants and displayed/calculated in `Europe/Vilnius`; local dates are not derived from a fixed UTC offset. Provisional scheduled check-in is **15:00 Europe/Vilnius** (D12). Define `A` as arrival date at local 15:00, `D = A − 14 calendar days` at local 15:00, and `W = A − 21 calendar days` at local 15:00. The free-cancellation cutoff is `D`: a guest cancellation committed at `t < D` is free; `t >= D` is late. The balance is timely if provider-verified successful payment occurred at `t <= D`. The overdue notice becomes eligible after `D` if unpaid; grace ends at `G = D + 24 elapsed hours`, not necessarily at local 15:00 the next day. Accept a verified balance during grace; reconcile again before cancellation at/after `G`. Booking eligibility for 30% versus 100% is fixed at the server-side instant `t` when the accepted quote, terms and checkout command are durably recorded with acquisition of the initial inventory claim. This is **not** the later Stripe Session creation time: `t < D` gets the 30% advance, while `t >= D` requires 100% immediately. If a checkout remains open across `D`, preserve its disclosed quote and payment split, but immediately after initial confirmation the balance is due; the UI must show this risk before checkout and prompt balance payment. This boundary case needs a dedicated implementation test.

Examples: for arrival **2027-04-10 15:00 EEST (12:00 UTC)**, `W` is **2027-03-20 15:00 EET (13:00 UTC)** and `D` is **2027-03-27 15:00 EET (13:00 UTC)**. `G` is **2027-03-28 16:00 EEST (13:00 UTC)**: DST advances during the 24 elapsed hours. For arrival **2027-11-13 15:00 EET (13:00 UTC)**, `D` is **2027-10-30 15:00 EEST (12:00 UTC)** and `G` is **2027-10-31 14:00 EET (12:00 UTC)** after fall-back. Use the timezone database; do not add `14 × 24h` to compute `D`.

Every amount is nonnegative integer EUR cents. Snapshot line items, discounts, configured taxes/fees once specified, total, policy/terms version, locale, and the presented amount/deadline. Reject a quote if its rules cannot produce a valid total. Use one documented deterministic rounding step: `advance = floor((total_cents × 30 + 50) / 100)` (round 30% to nearest cent, half up); `balance = total_cents − advance`. For a 10,001-cent total, advance is 3,000 cents and balance 7,001 cents. For `t >= D`, initial due is `total_cents` and balance is zero. Never independently round 70%, and never use floating point. No price change after acceptance alters a booked snapshot; adjustments require an explicit audited new financial event and applicable guest consent.

## 4. Hold and checkout creation

The initial portfolio restricts Checkout to **card** in Stripe test mode. Do not enable dynamic methods or delayed/asynchronous methods until a separate pending-settlement inventory policy is approved and tested (D19). Card authentication can still be delayed or fail, so only verified success confirms anything. Explicit `payment_method_types: ['card']` and `mode: payment`; assert EUR amount, test mode, metadata/`client_reference_id` correlation, and no automatic promotion/price changes. Stripe documents configurable Session expiry at **30 minutes to 24 hours**; use a 30-minute Checkout window measured from **provider Session creation**, and persist the provider-returned creation and expiry instants, not assumed local values. The blocking `CREATING` inventory claim starts earlier, at database acquisition. A checkout return page is read-only status and may request reconciliation; it never marks payment successful.

Safe initial sequence:

1. Validate request and immutable quote; require explicit accepted terms and payment obligation in the chosen `lt`/`en`/`de` language. Generate stable booking draft, hold and payment-attempt IDs plus operation idempotency key.
2. In one database transaction acquire an inventory-blocking `CREATING` hold and pending booking, snapshot quote/terms, and record a session-creation job. The same user command key returns the same draft/attempt. If a conflicting claim exists, reject without calling Stripe.
3. Worker creates one Stripe Session with a persistent Stripe idempotency key for that attempt. Do not hold a database transaction open across the provider call. Persist Session ID, returned creation/expiry instants, requested amount and URL; transition hold to `ACTIVE` only while the claim remains owned. Return the URL only after persistence succeeds. If provider creation or persistence is uncertain, reconcile identified provider objects and retry with the **same key only within the safe API v1 key-retention window**; after that, follow the quarantine process in §7. Do not create a fresh untracked checkout. A watchdog for a stuck `CREATING` job may release it only after authoritatively determining that no usable or paid provider Session exists; unresolved provider state remains blocking and raises an alert.
4. The hold retains exclusive inventory through `ACTIVE` and `RECONCILING`. At Session expiry or abandonment, retrieve Session and linked PaymentIntent, request provider expiration if still open, and reconcile. If the authoritative Session is still open, unpaid, unexpired and no payment is processing, and the claim is still owned, return `RECONCILING → ACTIVE` with the matching attempt `RECONCILING → OPEN`; return the same usable Session URL. Release only after provider state is terminal unpaid/expired and no payment success is in flight. If uncertain, keep `RECONCILING` and alert; do not strand it silently.
5. A repeated checkout command for the same draft returns the current usable Session. To start a new attempt, first terminally close/expire and reconcile the prior attempt; then atomically replace the hold or create a new one if availability still exists. Distinct attempts cannot both be usable for the same obligation. A second payment for an already satisfied obligation is a duplicate collection to refund, never extra booking credit.

Provider Session creation may occur minutes after claim acquisition, so the inventory claim's total blocking time can exceed the 30-minute Checkout window even without a failure. After nominal Session expiry, reconciliation may extend blocking time further. The authoritative release condition is terminal provider reconciliation, not merely a clock comparison: **no unconditional release at 30 minutes**. A successful payment after the nominal hold window may confirm only if the original claim is still exclusively held and the booking remains eligible. If released and another claim occupies the dates, record the money as **unallocatable**, do not evict that booking, notify operations and guest, and issue a traceable full refund. If dates happen to be free after release, do not silently resurrect an expired booking; require an explicit new guest checkout/offer or refund. This keeps confirmation deterministic.

## 5. Booking and payment lifecycle

The pending booking is `AWAITING_INITIAL_PAYMENT` while its hold blocks inventory. A verified successful initial payment of the exact expected attempt, with claim still owned, atomically marks the attempt succeeded, records a payment ledger credit, transfers the claim from hold to booking, and sets booking `CONFIRMED`. If an advance was paid, payment position is `BALANCE_DUE`; if full price was paid, `PAID_IN_FULL`. A failed/expired attempt leaves the booking pending only while another valid attempt/claim exists; otherwise close it `EXPIRED`. Failed initial payment does not confirm. Booking status remains `CONFIRMED` when balance is overdue; payment position becomes `OVERDUE`, then returns to `PAID_IN_FULL` on verified payment.

Create a balance Checkout attempt for exactly the unpaid scheduled balance, subject to idempotency and one usable attempt per obligation. A balance payment does not take a new hold: the confirmed booking already blocks dates. Pay-in-full notification follows verified payment only. If a payment succeeds after a booking was cancelled, classify it as unallocatable and refund; never reopen the booking or reclaim dates automatically. Refunds do not erase historical payment success; keep gross payments and provider-confirmed refunds as separate ledger facts.

`W` warning, `D` due notice, and post-`D` final overdue warning are scheduled from the snapped booking calendar and payment position. A warning is eligible only for a **confirmed** advance booking with balance outstanding at `t < D`: if confirmation occurs before `W`, send at `W`; if it occurs at/after `W` but before `D`, send once promptly after confirmation as a short-notice balance warning stating the **actual** remaining time and exact deadline. Neither case promises that seven days remain when sent. If confirmation occurs at/after `D`, or the balance has been paid, suppress the warning; an unsent queued warning becomes `SUPPRESSED` when obsolete. At/after `G`, scheduler finds candidates, locks them, reconciles all relevant Stripe attempts/PaymentIntents (including open/in-flight sessions), and checks whether a successful balance payment occurred by `D` or during grace. A verified success during grace preserves the booking and clears overdue. Any uncertain/processing provider result blocks automatic cancellation for review/retry. Expire open checkout where possible, then reconcile again before an atomic `CANCELLED_NONPAYMENT` transition and inventory release. A late-arriving webhook for a payment that succeeded by `D` must not cause a false cancellation; compare authoritative provider success time, not webhook arrival time. If a success is discovered after release despite this barrier, enter an operational incident: do not steal inventory; if still possible and guest consents, offer a new booking, otherwise refund all unallocatable money. Record the cause and notification correction. No automatic no-show penalty is defined.

## 6. Guest, non-payment, and provider cancellation

Cancellation request authorization and decisive instant are recorded server-side under booking lock. A guest request at `t < D` atomically changes booking to `CANCELLED_GUEST`, releases inventory and creates a zero-charge assessment and refund obligation for all captured funds. At `t >= D`, atomically cancel and release inventory, create `ASSESSMENT_PENDING`, and keep any collected money in an unsettled liability position until a documented assessment. Do **not** automatically retain the advance or refund only the balance. This allows rebooking/avoided-cost evidence to be included. Guest-facing language states the maximum, the review and expected update; the exact review SLA and live refund deadline need legal/policy approval.

For a late cancellation let `T = total booking price`, `P = sum of verified captured payments`, `C = final supportable charge`. Require `0 <= C <= floor(T × 30 / 100)` and `C <= documented supportable net loss`; record evidence, actual avoided costs, replacement revenue attributable to the same dates, relevant legal constraints, reviewer and rationale. A provisional assessment cannot be treated as final money retained. Once approved, `refund_due = max(0, P − C)` and `additional_charge = max(0, C − P)`; **no new post-cancellation collection is authorized** in this milestone, so an amount above `P` is recorded for review, not charged. If evidence later reduces `C`, create an immutable assessment revision and incremental refund `max(0, P − revised_C − previously_confirmed_refunds − pending_refund_amounts)`; never increase a final charge after communicating/refunding it without a separately approved rule. A successful rebooking is evidence for reassessment, not an automatic fixed refund formula; use actual recovered value and overlapping dates, avoid double counting, and document allocation. For example, `T=€400, P=€400, C=€80` yields €320 refund; if later mitigation lowers `C` to €20, refund an additional €60. If `P=€120`, `C=€80`, refund €40.

Provider-attributable cancellation releases inventory and creates a refund obligation for **all** verified guest payments, irrespective of the payment split. Any additional compensation is a separate policy requiring Lithuanian legal review; do not encode it as an automatic amount. The approved brief does not specify a non-payment cancellation charge. The conservative **engineering default proposed for this milestone** is to refund any captured advance and other captured money; adopting a charge would require an explicit, legally reviewed policy. In particular, the system must not quietly keep the advance. To avoid refunding money that is still being reconciled, calculate refund obligation from verified captures and rerun reconciliation before sending each provider refund.

Refund requests use an internal refund ID, a unique provider idempotency key, target original payment/charge, amount and reason. Allocate across captures without exceeding each refundable amount. `REQUESTED`/`PROCESSING` is not refunded; only provider-confirmed success becomes ledger debit and triggers guest confirmation. A failed refund stays owed and retryable with the same logical key **only while Stripe's API v1 key-retention window is safe**, or with a documented replacement attempt after authoritative provider reconciliation. An unknown result at/after that boundary is quarantined under §7; it is not a confirmed failure and cannot be sent again blindly. Never create two live refund operations for the same obligation; do not over-refund. If refund eligibility changes while a refund is in flight, reconcile that attempt before computing any incremental adjustment.

## 7. Provider events, reconciliation, and failure recovery

Persist internal attempt ID, Stripe Session ID, PaymentIntent ID when known, Stripe Event ID, provider refund ID, environment, amount and currency. Unique constraints on these IDs and logical obligation keys prevent duplicate effects. Verify Stripe signature over raw webhook body with the test endpoint secret; reject invalid signatures and wrong environment/account. Durably record verified events before acknowledging; if durable storage fails, return a retryable error. Queue processing and deduplicate Event ID, then reconcile the referenced current Session/PaymentIntent/Refund rather than relying on delivery order or event timestamp ordering. Stripe explicitly warns that delivery order is not guaranteed and duplicates occur ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)).

For the chosen card flow, `checkout.session.completed` is evidence that Checkout finished, **not by itself proof that every possible method settled**. Confirm only after checking Session `payment_status=paid` and linked PaymentIntent `status=succeeded`, amount received, currency EUR, expected IDs/metadata, environment and payment identity. A `payment_intent.succeeded` event can initiate the same reconciliation before a Session event. `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`/`failed`, if unexpectedly received, must enter reconciliation/quarantine, never bypass the restriction. `payment_intent.payment_failed`, Session expiry, and provider refund events update their own state only after current-object verification. An unknown event type is logged and acknowledged without financial transition. The initial card-only decision limits the long pending window but does not remove authentication delays or late events.

For timing disputes, preserve provider event/charge evidence of when success occurred. If precise success time cannot be proved from authoritative provider records, do not automatically cancel; flag manual review. Reconciliation runs on webhook receipt, return-page status fetch, scheduled expiry and nonpayment jobs, refund jobs, and periodic sweep of open/stale attempts. External API calls use idempotency keys for create/refund; database transitions use transaction locks and compare-and-set state/version checks. Never put provider calls inside a database transaction. Store a durable command/outbox before calling Stripe and reconcile unknown outcomes with the same operation key **only while that key can safely deduplicate**. Network timeout means **unknown**, not failed. Alert on orphan Session, mismatched amount, unexpected live-mode event, prolonged reconciliation, failed refund, and undelivered critical notice.

Stripe [API v1 idempotency guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) says keys may be pruned after they are at least **24 hours old**; reuse after pruning creates a new request. Record first-submission time, key, request parameters and provider request/correlation IDs. Once the first submission is at/over 24 hours old, or its age is unknown, **stop automatic POST retries with that key** for unresolved Session creation or refund creation. Move the operation to an audited reconciliation quarantine (`RECONCILING` for a hold/attempt, `PROCESSING` for a refund), preserve inventory blocking or refund liability, and alert authorized operations. Identify any existing Session/refund through stored IDs, verified events, associated PaymentIntent/charge and provider records; inspect payment/refund status and amount. A missing search result alone is not proof that the POST never succeeded. If authoritative evidence identifies an object, attach and reconcile its actual state; an open, unpaid and unexpired Session with an owned claim can take the recovery transitions above. If evidence establishes the operation did not occur, an authorized operator may close the old command and create a **new** attempt with a new key after checking inventory or remaining refundable balance. If neither outcome is provable, keep it quarantined for operational investigation; do not create a duplicate Session or refund and do not silently release the claim. Record evidence, actor and resolution. This is a failure-recovery rule, not a new commercial term.

## 8. Notification contract

Notification creation is an outbox write in the same transaction as the triggering domain event. Templates are semantically equivalent in `lt`/`en`/`de`, using the booking's chosen locale and property-local deadlines with zone labels. Only synthetic guest addresses in portfolio mode. Email transport is sandbox/test configured; an admin-only retry may be offered. Each row uses a unique idempotency key `booking-id:event-type:domain-event-id-or-scheduled-instant:recipient:locale`; send attempts reference that row and provider message ID. Scheduler deduplicates by key. Retry transient transport failures with bounded exponential backoff and jitter (e.g. 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 12 h; then alert/manual retry); permanent failures alert immediately. A sending timeout is uncertain: query provider/message state where possible before retry, and use provider idempotency if supported. Delivery failure never changes booking or money. The matrix's **audit** column identifies the event logged in addition to the universal outbox/attempt/provider response trail.

If scheduler runs after a notice's useful window, re-evaluate current payment/booking status and suppress stale reminders with an audited suppression. Specifically, never send a balance warning at/after `D`, even if it was queued earlier; the `D` notice governs then. Do not send a due/overdue notice after payment confirmation or a cancellation notice twice. Notification links use scoped, short-lived manage-booking tokens; do not include raw payment credentials or sensitive personal data in logs.

## 9. Authorization and audit boundary

Guests may quote publicly, but booking creation requires anti-abuse controls, validated input and explicit terms acceptance. A guest may inspect/pay/cancel only a booking authorized by a short-lived, unpredictable, scoped emailed token; possession of an ID alone grants nothing. Rotate/revoke tokens on sensitive changes. Administrative inventory blocks, provider cancellations, assessments, refunds, resends and manual payment corrections require authenticated role-specific authority, reason and actor audit; refund initiation uses stronger finance permission and cannot be performed through a guest token. Webhooks have only verified provider-event authority. Scheduled jobs have scoped service identity. Avoid personally identifying data in event details.

Append immutable audit events for quote acceptance, hold/booking transitions, provider calls/results, reconciliation snapshots, payment credits, assessment revisions/evidence, refund requests/outcomes, inventory release, notices and authorization failures. Include actor type/ID, UTC and local context, operation/idempotency key, prior/new state, relevant amounts, provider IDs and reason. No notification handler may independently change financial state. Run invariant/ledger reconciliation reports; repair through an audited corrective action, never by editing history.
